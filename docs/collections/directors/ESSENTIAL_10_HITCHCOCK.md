### Rationale, one sentence each

1. **Vertigo** - Obsession, false identity and a film told almost entirely through one man's point of view, with the dolly-zoom and Bernard Herrmann's score; the highest-placed Hitchcock in the 2022 Sight and Sound poll (second overall) and the clearest case that his thrillers are also formal art.
2. **Psycho** - Removed its star at the midpoint and rebuilt the thriller and the horror film around a cut-up shower sequence that is still taught; first on IMDb votes and rating in this list and near the top on RT.
3. **Rear Window** - An entire film staged from one apartment, making the audience's looking the subject; the highest Tomatometer of his four best-known films and the most reviewed (99% from 138 reviews).
4. **North by Northwest** - The wrong-man chase scaled to a continent, with the crop-duster and Mount Rushmore set pieces; the template for the glossy international adventure thriller.
5. **Notorious** - A spy romance in which the camera does the storytelling, from the long crane shot down to the key in Ingrid Bergman's hand to the extended kiss shot around Production Code limits.
6. **Strangers on a Train** - An exchange-of-murders premise built on visual doubling, ending in a fairground carousel climax; 98% on RT and one of his tightest constructions.
7. **Shadow of a Doubt** - Menace placed inside a small American town and a family home, with a charming uncle as the threat; the only film in this list with a 100% Tomatometer on a sample above 50 reviews.
8. **The 39 Steps** - The British-period template for the innocent man on the run, cut for pace and wit; the blueprint he later rebuilt at scale as North by Northwest.
9. **The Birds** - Unexplained natural horror with no conventional score, using electronic sound instead; a late-career formal risk with 223,000 IMDb votes and 96% on RT.
10. **Rope** - Staged as apparently continuous takes in one apartment, a formal experiment that makes staging rather than cutting the drama; the closest call in the ten (see below).

**Near misses** (considered, not included):

- **Rebecca** (1940), TMDB 223, IMDb tt0032976: Best Picture winner with strong evidence (IMDb 8.0 from 162,951 votes; RT 98% from 105 reviews), but a Selznick-shaped production and the least Hitchcock-specific of his top-rated films; the closest call against Rope.
- **The Lady Vanishes** (1938), TMDB 940, IMDb tt0030341: Best of the British run alongside The 39 Steps and overlaps it.
- **Dial M for Murder** (1954), TMDB 521, IMDb tt0046912: High audience standing (IMDb 8.1) but a filmed play with a weaker Tomatometer (89%).
- **Lifeboat** (1944), TMDB 13321, IMDb tt0037017: Single-set formal exercise, overlaps Rope.
- **Spellbound** (1945), TMDB 4174, IMDb tt0038109: Dream sequence by Dali is notable, but the film as a whole is below the ten.
- **Frenzy** (1972), TMDB 573, IMDb tt0068611: Strong late return to form; not strong enough against the ten.

## Ranking method

The method is the one set out in [`ESSENTIAL_10_EXAMPLE.md`](./ESSENTIAL_10_EXAMPLE.md#ranking-method) and was applied unchanged: eligibility from TMDB crew credits, an evidence panel of IMDb rating and votes, RT Tomatometer and review count and a note on directing, tiers by directing achievement, order within a tier by written editorial judgement, and the top three flagged as signature films. IMDb and RT are never averaged or combined and are used only as ordinal evidence within this director's own filmography. Suggested sample floors are 10,000 IMDb votes and 20 RT reviews; below those a score is shown but carries little weight. Two additions follow the owner's decisions: documentaries and co-directed films are allowed where essential and flagged, and the list is shorter than ten where ten would be padding.

### Where the evidence and the order disagree (all deliberate)

- **Rope is #10 although Rebecca beats it on both lines** (Rebecca: IMDb 8.0, 162,951 votes; RT 98%, 105 reviews. Rope: IMDb 7.9, 167,383 votes; RT 93%, 58 reviews). Reason: the list is about directing achievement, and Rope's continuous-take experiment is a purer Hitchcock formal statement than Rebecca, which Selznick controlled. This is the swap an owner is most likely to want; Rebecca is first reserve.
- **The middle band (#5 to #9) is close to interchangeable.** Notorious (96% from 51 reviews; IMDb 7.9), Strangers on a Train (98% from 56; 7.9), Shadow of a Doubt (100% from 54; 7.7), The 39 Steps (96% from 53; 7.6) and The Birds (96% from 70; 7.6, but 223,459 votes) sit within two points of each other on RT and a few tenths on IMDb. Their order is editorial: Notorious and Strangers on a Train for visual command, Shadow of a Doubt for the American-small-town turn, The 39 Steps for its template role, The Birds for its formal risk.
- **Vertigo is #1 although Psycho and Rear Window have higher IMDb ratings (8.5 and 8.4 against 8.2)** and Rear Window has the higher Tomatometer (99% against 95%). Reason: Sight and Sound placed Vertigo second of all films in 2022, and its formal case (subjective camera, dolly-zoom) is the most consequential.

**Known uncertainties**

- Sight and Sound positions come from the BFI page (see sources); positions for Strangers on a Train, Shadow of a Doubt, The 39 Steps and Rope were not seen in the portions read.
- Rope (#10) versus Rebecca is an editorial call against the scores (see above).
- RT 'The 39 Steps' page is /m/1000121-39_steps (the plain /m/39_steps is a 2008 remake); 'The Birds' is /m/1002448-birds; 'Notorious' is /m/1015287-notorious (the plain slug is a 1992 TV film); /m/rebecca is the 2020 remake.
- IMDb ids were matched from the dataset by title, year and runtime; every TMDB runtime agrees with IMDb to within 1 minute.
- Review counts and vote counts move daily; these are snapshots.
- TMDB film pages carry no IMDb link, so IMDb ids were matched by title, year and runtime; confirm via the TMDB API `external_ids` field when implementing.
- Alternate titles in the ledger come from IMDb primary and original titles and the TMDB display titles, not from TMDB's alternative-titles tab.

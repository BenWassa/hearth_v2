### Rationale, one sentence each

1. **2001: A Space Odyssey** - Made the science-fiction film a vehicle for pure image and music, with almost no dialogue, a staged sense of scale and a vision of technology that still sets the terms for the genre; it carries the strongest combined evidence in his filmography.
2. **Dr. Strangelove or: How I Learned to Stop Worrying and Love the Bomb** - A Cold War nuclear-crisis thriller turned into black comedy, built from three rigorously separated spaces (war room, bomber, base) and a multi-role performance by Peter Sellers; the highest Tomatometer of his major films.
3. **A Clockwork Orange** - A violent satire of state and individual control whose visual design, ironic use of classical music and rigidly composed wide-angle images made it one of the most imitated and argued-over films of its decade; ranked above The Shining on influence even though the audience score is a little lower.
4. **The Shining** - Early extended use of the Steadicam turns a hotel into a maze of unstable space and a horror film into a study of a family under pressure; first on IMDb among his films, lowest of the top five on the Tomatometer, a gap this ranking treats as a settled dispute about genre, not about craft.
5. **Paths of Glory** - First-World-War court-martial drama with long tracking shots through the trenches and a mercilessly clear structure; the film that established him as a major director and still scores near the top on both evidence lines.
6. **Barry Lyndon** - Eighteenth-century picaresque shot largely by natural and candle light with very fast lenses; the film that most clearly shows his command of period image-making.
7. **Full Metal Jacket** - A two-part war film (boot camp, then Vietnam) that reads as a study of how violence is trained and then released; strong on both evidence lines.
8. **The Killing** - Heist thriller told out of sequence from several viewpoints; the first fully realised Kubrick film and a formal model for later crime cinema.
9. **Spartacus** - A large-scale epic directed after Kubrick replaced the original director and over which he had little creative control; included for the strength of the staging, the ensemble and the evidence, and flagged as the least personal film in the ten.
10. **Eyes Wide Shut** - His final film: a slow, dreamlike study of jealousy and desire in a stylised New York; the closest call in the ten (see below).

**Near misses** (considered, not included):

- Lolita (1962; TMDB 802, tt0056193): Strong adaptation with an uneven tone; ranked just behind Eyes Wide Shut on audience standing and narrower influence. IMDb 7.5 (117,843), RT 83% (92).
- Killer's Kiss (1955; TMDB 10056, tt0048254): Short, early noir that matters mainly as a step toward The Killing. IMDb 6.5 (28,902), RT 81% (review count not read).

No documentaries or co-directed films in this list. Fear and Desire exists in a restored version; the IMDb and RT figures above are for the film as listed.

## Ranking method

Applied exactly as in the Kurosawa worked example ([`ESSENTIAL_10_EXAMPLE.md`](./ESSENTIAL_10_EXAMPLE.md#ranking-method)): eligibility, longlist and evidence panel (at least 10,000 IMDb votes), each source read on its own terms (never averaged), tier by directing achievement, order within tier by editorial judgement, top three = Tier 1, freeze and version, second-reader review. Changes for this director: documentaries and co-directed films are allowed where essential and flagged (owner decision), and the list may be shorter than ten. Note that for the longlist films outside the ten, the RT score was read only where the film was a plausible candidate; the others carry an IMDb figure only.

### Where the evidence and the order disagree (all deliberate)

- **The Shining has the highest IMDb rating (8.4, joint with Paths of Glory) and 1.25 million votes, but sits at #4** with the lowest Tomatometer of the top five (75%). Reason: the ranking weighs directing achievement and influence; the critic dispute is about how horror is judged. It is the most likely film to be moved up by an owner who leans on audience standing.
- **Paths of Glory (IMDb 8.4, RT 96%) is #5** though it matches or beats A Clockwork Orange and The Shining on both sources. Reason: the first three are the films with the largest claim on formal innovation and influence; this is the closest call for the third place and could reasonably be swapped.
- **Eyes Wide Shut (#10) has the lowest IMDb rating in the ten (7.5)** and a 79% Tomatometer that is similar to Barry Lyndon and The Shining. It edges Lolita (7.5, 83%, 92 reviews) on audience size (445,052 vs 117,843 votes) and on being his last film. Owner could prefer Lolita.
- **Spartacus (#9)** is on the list despite limited creative control; a reader who wants only fully personal work would replace it with Lolita.

**Known uncertainties**

- IMDb ids were matched in the IMDb dataset by original title, year and runtime, not read from a TMDB external_ids field; confirm with the TMDB API external_ids at implementation.
- RT review counts are a single read on the stated date via a text fetcher.
- IMDb title pages could not be read (HTTP 403); ratings and votes come from the official dataset (not cross-checked against the live pages), whose licence is non-commercial only.
- TMDB person-page years displayed by the fetch tool were sometimes offset for other directors; for Kubrick all years match the original release years used here.
- RT pages show US release years for some films; the ledger uses original release years (per-film notes in the JSON ledger).
- Review counts and vote counts move daily; these are snapshots.

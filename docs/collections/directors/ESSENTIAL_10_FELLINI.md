### Rationale, one sentence each

1. **8½** - A film about a director's creative block that became the model for self-portrait cinema, with dream and memory moving freely through one man's day; 98% on RT and the highest-placed Fellini in the 2022 Sight and Sound poll (=31).
2. **La Dolce Vita** - Seven nights and days of a journalist in Rome, the Palme d'Or winner (Cannes 1960) that put celebrity culture and the paparazzi on screen; 75 RT reviews at 95%.
3. **La Strada** - The neorealist road tragedy of Gelsomina and Zampano with Nino Rota's score; the first of his Foreign Language Oscar winners and 97% from 77 reviews.
4. **Nights of Cabiria** - Giulietta Masina's Rome prostitute whose faith survives repeated betrayal; Foreign Language Oscar winner with 100% from 41 reviews and 8.1 on IMDb.
5. **Amarcord** - Memory of a Romagna town living under fascism, comic and elegiac; Foreign Language Oscar winner and the clearest statement of his late autobiographical mode.
6. **I Vitelloni** - Five idle young men in a provincial town, the bridge from neorealism to his own autobiographical manner; 100% from 28 reviews.
7. **Juliet of the Spirits** - His first full-length colour feature, a hallucinatory study of a wife's imagination with Giulietta Masina; the clearest case for his colour design.
8. **Fellini Satyricon** - A hallucinatory, stylised vision of ancient Rome built on constructed sets, among the most visually uncompromising films he made; mixed evidence (6.8 on IMDb, 74% on RT).
9. **The White Sheik** - A farce about a bride lost in Rome chasing a photo-romance idol, showing his comic gift before the major films; evidence is thin (7,667 IMDb votes, 13 RT reviews) so it is the closest call and the reason this list is nine long.

### Credit, format and version flags

- **The White Sheik (1952)**: Sole director per TMDB crew (Director, Screenplay, Story). His first solo-credited feature; Variety Lights (1950, TMDB 43389) was co-directed with Alberto Lattuada and is not ranked.

**Near misses** (considered, not included):

- **Fellini's Roma** (1972), TMDB 11035, IMDb tt0069191: A city portrait with strong passages, but RT 65% from 17 reviews and overlaps 8½'s autobiographical mode; first reserve for a tenth place.
- **Il Bidone (The Swindle)** (1955), TMDB 36102, IMDb tt0047876: Minor work between La Strada and Nights of Cabiria.
- **Variety Lights** (1950), TMDB 43389, IMDb tt0042692: Co-directed with Alberto Lattuada; excluded from ranking as a joint credit, and only 9 RT reviews.
- **Fellini's Casanova** (1976), TMDB 42236, IMDb tt0074291: Spectacular but RT 64% from 11 reviews.
- **And the Ship Sails On** (1983), TMDB 5622, IMDb tt0087188: Late fantasy, not stronger than the nine.
- **Boccaccio '70 / Spirits of the Dead (anthology segments)**, TMDB 45267, IMDb tt0055805: Anthology segments (Toby Dammit, The Temptation of Dr. Antonio); excluded as non-features.

## Ranking method

The method is the one set out in [`ESSENTIAL_10_EXAMPLE.md`](./ESSENTIAL_10_EXAMPLE.md#ranking-method) and was applied unchanged: eligibility from TMDB crew credits, an evidence panel of IMDb rating and votes, RT Tomatometer and review count and a note on directing, tiers by directing achievement, order within a tier by written editorial judgement, and the top three flagged as signature films. IMDb and RT are never averaged or combined and are used only as ordinal evidence within this director's own filmography. Suggested sample floors are 10,000 IMDb votes and 20 RT reviews; below those a score is shown but carries little weight. Two additions follow the owner's decisions: documentaries and co-directed films are allowed where essential and flagged, and the list is shorter than ten where ten would be padding.

### Where the evidence and the order disagree (all deliberate)

- **La Strada is #3 although Nights of Cabiria has the higher IMDb rating (8.1 against 8.0) and a 100% Tomatometer.** Reason: La Strada is the more widely seen and influential, with 70,763 IMDb votes against 57,937 and a larger critic sample (77 against 41); the gap is small and the pair are interchangeable.
- **Amarcord (#5) has a lower Tomatometer (87% from 47 reviews) than I Vitelloni (#6; 100% from 28 reviews) and the same IMDb rating (7.8).** Reason: the order of the middle band rests on scale, influence and Oscar record (Amarcord won the Foreign Language Oscar; I Vitelloni did not).
- **Juliet of the Spirits, Fellini Satyricon and The White Sheik (#7 to #9)** carry weaker evidence (IMDb 7.4, 6.8 and 7.2; RT 78%, 74% and 100% from 13 reviews). They are kept because the next candidates (Roma, Il Bidone, Casanova) are weaker still. The list is deliberately nine rather than ten: no tenth film clears the same bar.

**Known uncertainties**

- The RT release year on the La Dolce Vita, Fellini Satyricon and The White Sheik pages could not be parsed by the extraction script, so rtListedYear is null for them.
- Variety Lights (TMDB 43389) is co-directed with Alberto Lattuada and Boccaccio '70 / Spirits of the Dead contain Fellini segments; none is ranked.
- IMDb ids matched by title, year and runtime; 8½ is 139 minutes on TMDB and 138 on IMDb; La Dolce Vita 174 on both; Juliet of the Spirits is 148 on TMDB and 137 on IMDb (a single entry on both, so the gap is treated as a cut or listing difference).
- TMDB year for La Dolce Vita is 1960, Amarcord 1973; the BFI page lists Amarcord as 1972 and Sight and Sound's year therefore differs by one.
- Review counts and vote counts move daily; these are snapshots.
- TMDB film pages carry no IMDb link, so IMDb ids were matched by title, year and runtime; confirm via the TMDB API `external_ids` field when implementing.
- Alternate titles in the ledger come from IMDb primary and original titles and the TMDB display titles, not from TMDB's alternative-titles tab.

**Director identity:** Hayao Miyazaki, TMDB person [608](https://www.themoviedb.org/person/608). He directed 12 theatrical features (1979-2023). TMDB also lists shorts, Ghibli Museum films, TV series, specials and an unreleased project under his credits; these are excluded, as are Sherlock Hound and Lupin TV material. Each film in the ten is listed with the Director role and the IMDb dataset names him as sole director. Because only 12 features exist, two features are left out.

### Rationale, one sentence each

1. **Spirited Away** - Dreamlike bathhouse fantasy that became the highest-grossing film in Japanese history on its release and won the Oscar for Best Animated Feature; top on IMDb (8.6) and the strongest combined evidence.
2. **My Neighbor Totoro** - A film with almost no conflict, about two girls and nature spirits in a Japanese village; the most-loved Ghibli film and the clearest statement of his sensibility.
3. **Princess Mononoke** - His epic of the conflict between industry and nature, with the deepest world-building and the biggest action; second-highest IMDb rating among his films (8.3) and a landmark of adult animation.
4. **Kiki's Delivery Service** - A gentle coming-of-age story about independence and burnout; the highest Tomatometer of his major films (98%).
5. **Castle in the Sky** - His first film for Studio Ghibli, a pure adventure of airships, pirates and a lost city; the model for much that followed (RT sample only 28 reviews).
6. **The Wind Rises** - A biographical drama about the designer of the Zero fighter, treated as a meditation on creation and complicity; his most personal and most contested film, and an Oscar nominee for Best Animated Feature.
7. **Howl's Moving Castle** - A romantic fantasy with flowing, hand-drawn invention and an anti-war undercurrent; higher on IMDb (8.2) than The Wind Rises but lower on the Tomatometer (87%).
8. **Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind** - The post-apocalyptic ecological epic that made the Ghibli name and set up the themes of Mononoke; made before Ghibli was founded.
9. **The Boy and the Heron** - A late, enigmatic and dreamlike film with a different structure from his earlier work; won the Oscar for Best Animated Feature (2024) and has the largest critic sample in the ten (276 reviews); lowest IMDb rating among the ten (7.3).
10. **Porco Rosso** - A wry romantic adventure about a pilot cursed to look like a pig; the closest call in the ten (see below).

## Ranking method

Applied exactly as in the Kurosawa worked example ([`ESSENTIAL_10_EXAMPLE.md`](./ESSENTIAL_10_EXAMPLE.md#ranking-method)): eligibility, longlist and evidence panel (at least 10,000 IMDb votes), each source read on its own terms (never averaged), tier by directing achievement, order within tier by editorial judgement, top three = Tier 1, freeze and version, second-reader review. Changes for this director: documentaries and co-directed films are allowed where essential and flagged (owner decision), and the list may be shorter than ten. Note that for the longlist films outside the ten, the RT score was read only where the film was a plausible candidate; the others carry an IMDb figure only.

### Where the evidence and the order disagree (all deliberate)

- **Porco Rosso (#10) has IMDb 7.7 and RT 96% from only 23 reviews, while Ponyo has IMDb 7.6 and RT 91% from 173 reviews and The Castle of Cagliostro has RT 95% from 21 reviews.** All three are close; Porco Rosso is kept for its narrative control. Ponyo and Cagliostro are the alternates, and the owner could reasonably prefer either.
- **The Boy and the Heron (#9) has the lowest IMDb rating in the ten (7.3) but the largest critic sample (96% from 276 reviews) and an Oscar.** Ranked below the others because it is a late, more opaque film.
- **Howl's Moving Castle (IMDb 8.2) is #7, below The Wind Rises (IMDb 7.8, RT 89%).** Reason: The Wind Rises is the more singular and ambitious work; Howl scores higher on the audience line and lower on the Tomatometer. Easy swap.
- **Castle in the Sky (28 reviews), Nausicaä (23) and Porco Rosso (23) have thin RT samples**, only a few above the 20-review floor; their percentages are weak evidence and their places rest more on the editorial case and on IMDb.

**Known uncertainties**

- IMDb ids were matched in the IMDb dataset by original title, year and runtime, not read from a TMDB external_ids field; confirm with the TMDB API external_ids at implementation.
- RT review counts are a single read on the stated date via a text fetcher.
- IMDb title pages could not be read (HTTP 403); ratings and votes come from the official dataset (not cross-checked against the live pages), whose licence is non-commercial only.
- TMDB id 81 (Nausicaä) page title/runtime reflect the US 'Warriors of the Wind' cut; the ledger keeps the id (it is the only Nausicaä id listed on his TMDB page) and flags the version question.
- Year displayed on the TMDB person page was shifted for several films (for example Totoro shown as 1986); ids and titles were checked and the ledger uses original release years confirmed from IMDb.
- RT pages show US release years for some films; the ledger uses original release years (per-film notes in the JSON ledger).
- Review counts and vote counts move daily; these are snapshots.

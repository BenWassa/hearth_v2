# Collections: Worlds on Tonight

Implemented from [Issue #6](https://github.com/BenWassa/hearth_v2/issues/6), its [approved classification decisions](https://github.com/BenWassa/hearth_v2/issues/6#issuecomment-6402151427), and the user's subsequent navigation correction. Reviewed 2026-10-26.

## Navigation

**Tonight → World → saga → film.** Tonight's Worlds section is one finite horizontal brand rail showing every World with something on the shelf, in fixed editorial order: Pixar, Marvel, Star Wars, Wizarding World, Disney, DreamWorks, DC, Studio Ghibli. Worlds with nothing on the shelf are skipped. The 7:2 (3.5:1) tiles at `clamp(11rem, 56vw, 15rem)` wide (about 176 to 240px) show one tile and a clear peek of the next on a phone. Tiles are never shorter than 44px; at 176px wide, a tile is 50px tall. The roughly 4:1 cards crop only a sliver at the sides. The rail is a plain scroll-snap row with native scrolling and arrow keys: no looping, cloned tiles, autoplay or auto-advance, and no edge fade needed. The tiles sit after the first suggestion rail so decision content comes first, under a small uppercase Worlds label without an icon, each using designer-made banner cards (`public/assets/worlds/<id>/card.webp`, about 780x190, roughly 4:1), with the World's logo baked into a rich tonal gradient and a soft corner highlight. Never overlay a separate logo or apply per-World wordmark width fractions, which would duplicate the name. Each tile is a button with the World name as its accessible name; the image is decorative with empty alt text (`alt=""`). A warm stone background sits under the image, and the serif World name shows if the image fails. Further down, a finite, non-looping **Collections** row sits directly above The Classics (and still shows when Classics is absent): every eligible saga except those whose World is shown in the Worlds rail (they are reached through their World; the rail and the row read the same World list, so changing the Worlds shown changes the row automatically), as poster cards in recent-activity order with no counts. With every represented World in the rail, the row effectively shows only sagas in no World, ending with a neutral **All collections** tile (when more than three sagas are shown, or whenever any saga is left out of the row) that opens the full index, which still lists every saga. There is no Browse-all link. Even a World with one saga opens its World page.

World pages open with edge-to-edge illustrated hero artwork only: the art carries the World name, with no visible title. The `h1` stays as screen-reader text and as the visible fallback if the image fails; counts are screen-reader context, not visible. Below it, sagas flagged big by the editorial `rail` flag in the catalog get a finite horizontal poster rail of owned films only when at least two are owned, headed by the plain serif saga name without a chevron and a quiet right-aligned Details text button (44px target). Every other saga with owned films, including flagged sagas with fewer than two, appears in a two-column index of backdrop tiles with serif saga names and no counts, headed 'More <World name>' when rails exist, otherwise 'Sagas'. Other films, Shorts & specials and TV series rails follow when supplied metadata supports them. World pages have no search, sort, refresh or chips. A saga tile, a rail's Details button or a Tonight Collections saga card opens a saga page as a layered dialog: backdrop hero, serif title, owned films as a three-across poster grid in release order, then provider-listed films not on the shelf, then Shorts & specials. Sagas outside any World are found in the All collections index. Films without sequels are not invented sagas. Back from a World opened on Tonight returns directly to the same Tonight tile; The All collections index is reached only from the Tonight Collections row.

The full index is deliberately plain: a back control and the title, then every eligible saga (including ungrouped ones) as a row with a short progress line, in recent-activity order fixed for the visit. It has no search, sort, refresh, count or Worlds grid for now. Opening film details leaves the saga mounted underneath, so closing the film restores focus and scroll to the same saga control. Escape dismisses only the top dialog. New controls are at least 44px high; No additional dock tab is introduced. The Tonight Worlds rail, Tonight Collections row, World-page saga rails and the media rails are the horizontal rails here; each is finite and bounded (no looping, cloned cards, autoplay or infinite scroll). The saga tile index and the saga page poster grid are grids.

## Four distinct decisions

1. **Membership:** preserve canonical TMDB collection metadata on a film.
2. **World affiliation:** a versioned editorial collection/movie-ID mapping or validated explicit World metadata assigns a primary brand group.
3. **Collection eligibility:** the curated recognized-franchise catalog alone determines first-class saga visibility. There is no household Show/Hide control.
4. **Activity relevance:** meaningful watch/add events order browsing within the stable visit. Relevance never removes a represented World from Tonight.

`src/config/collectionCatalog.js` and `src/domain/media/collectionEligibility.js` are the pure classification boundary. Provider size, ratings, corporate ownership and raw title matching never promote a card. Missing metadata and unknown collections default safely to individual shelf films.

Approved examples include Avengers, Harry Potter, Fantastic Beasts, Skywalker Star Wars, Star Trek reboot, Narnia, Knives Out, Dune (2021), Toy Story, James Bond, Fast & Furious, Dark Knight and Back to the Future. Fast & Furious, Dark Knight and Back to the Future can qualify with one owned feature. Paddington and Ghostbusters are hidden by default even when multiple features are owned, unless the household overrides. James Bond is an anthology: Explore films replaces an invented Next up order.

## Household preferences (dormant)

There is no UI for household Show/Hide overrides, and the app opens no Firestore listener or read for them, so Collections never waits on, or fails because of, preference loading. Sagas and Worlds render immediately from the editorial catalog. The earlier backend (`useCollectionPreferences`, `services/firebase/collectionPreferences`, the `collection_preferences` block in `firestore.rules`, the emulator script and their tests) is intentionally left in the repository, unused, pending a decision to delete it.

## Worlds and truthful counts

The catalog supports Wizarding World, Marvel, Star Wars, Pixar, Disney, DC, DreamWorks Animation and Studio Ghibli. Disney means traditional Disney films, not every Disney-owned brand. Avengers is one child saga of Marvel; Dark Knight is a child of DC. Existing Disney IDs remain stable. Standalone affiliations use verified movie IDs or validated optional World IDs, never company-name guessing.

Canonical provider/type identities deduplicate shelf records. Explicit shorts/specials/compilations and positive runtimes <=40 minutes are excluded from feature progress; runtime is a fallback heuristic and unknown runtime is not proof of a short. Features, watched features, shorts and TV have separate counts. World membership does not create sequel relationships.

Saga pages distinguish owned features from provider listings. Provider shorts have a separate section and retain correct shelf ownership. Unknown provider format metadata is labeled as provider-listed films rather than a certified feature total. Missing provider data retains the household shelf; unknown totals never imply franchise completion or an owned next film. Opening another saga never briefly exposes the previous saga's provider parts.

## Activity, stability and providers

Movie unwatched→watched transitions transact against the current shared record and set `userState.markedWatchedAt`; undo clears it, and repeated same-status writes preserve it. `additionOrigin` distinguishes manual adds from imports. No historical watch dates are backfilled. Notes, refreshes, ratings, TV progress and general updatedAt do not create saga freshness.

Browser order uses meaningful last-event time with alphabetical/ID fallback. Tonight's World order is editorial and does not change with household activity. Positions freeze while browsing; progress and removals update live, and re-entering incorporates newer ordering. The pure activity-ranking functions remain tested, but Tonight renders Worlds rather than a capped saga recommendation preview.

Tonight performs no provider-count fan-out for classification or World counts. Saga metadata is requested on demand. Backend collection-part hydration is capped at three, retains partial base metadata on individual failures, and never falls back from a missing movie ID to an unrelated TV show. The optional small-saga count helper also retains its three-request FIFO/cache, but only certifies a feature count when format metadata supports it.

## Artwork and validation

Saga rows use existing TMDB collection artwork, lazy loading and w300 thumbnail crops. Tonight loads only the designer-made banner card at `public/assets/worlds/<id>/card.webp` per World (about 780x190, roughly 4:1), with the logo baked into a rich tonal gradient and a soft corner highlight; the World page keeps the illustrated cover artwork at natural 16:9, with a screen-reader `h1` that becomes visible if the image fails. The logos in these cards are designer mockup renditions of the studios' marks, not verified official files and not licensed. They are acceptable for private use, but must be replaced with permitted brand assets or removed before any public release. No scraped promotional art is used. TMDB imagery follows the app's existing attribution and terms.

Synthetic demo scenarios are available at `?demo=1&collectionsFixture=25` (also 0/1/50), with optional `dispersedWorlds=1`. Large/dispersed scenarios exercise unclassified metadata candidates rather than forcing them into an invented World. No household export or private watch history is used. See [validation, emulator checks and captures](docs/collections/VALIDATION.md) for final results and limitations.

# Legendary Directors: proposed roster of 12

**Issue:** [#60](https://github.com/BenWassa/hearth_v2/issues/60) · **Status:** Draft for owner review (first review gate) · **Researched:** 2026-11-13
**Companion:** [`ESSENTIAL_10_EXAMPLE.md`](./ESSENTIAL_10_EXAMPLE.md) (one complete worked list, Kurosawa)

## Selection basis

Chosen only for excellence in directing across world cinema: quality of the films, command of craft, formal innovation, influence on other filmmakers, and consistent achievement over a body of work. No demographic quotas and no personal attributes were used. The spread of countries and eras in the result is an outcome of the evidence, not a criterion. A further test was practical: a fixed Essential 10 should be fillable without padding.

Twelve is a hard cap, so excellent directors are left out. The alternates under each entry and the "close calls" list at the end are the nearest misses.

TMDB person ids were opened and the name and "Directing" department confirmed on 2026-11-13. Film-level credits for everyone except Kurosawa have not been audited yet (that is the next step after approval).

## The twelve

### 1. Akira Kurosawa (TMDB 5026) - Japan
*Rashomon* (1950) made contradictory testimony a structure and carried Japanese cinema to Western audiences (Golden Lion, Venice 1951). *Seven Samurai* (1954) fixed the template of the assembled-team adventure and was remade as *The Magnificent Seven*; *Ikiru* (1952) is the humane counterweight, a dying clerk's search for meaning; *Throne of Blood* (1957) and *Ran* (1985) recast Shakespeare through Noh and screen scale; *Yojimbo* (1961) became *A Fistful of Dollars* and *The Hidden Fortress* (1958) influenced *Star Wars*. Thirty films across five decades with a deep run of masterworks. **Alternates:** Yasujiro Ozu, Kenji Mizoguchi.

### 2. Alfred Hitchcock (TMDB 2636) - United Kingdom / United States
The grammar of suspense on screen: *The 39 Steps* (1935), *Shadow of a Doubt* (1943), *Rear Window* (1954), *Vertigo* (1958), *North by Northwest* (1959) and *Psycho* (1960) pair controlled point of view with images that still teach film schools (the cropped-in shower montage, the vertigo dolly-zoom). *Rope* (1948) staged as apparently continuous takes. *Vertigo* is regularly placed at or near the top of the Sight and Sound critics' poll. Unmatched consistency over fifty years. **Alternates:** Billy Wilder, Orson Welles.

### 3. Stanley Kubrick (TMDB 240) - United States / United Kingdom
Reworked a different genre almost every film and left each defining: *Paths of Glory* (1957), *Dr. Strangelove* (1964), *2001: A Space Odyssey* (1968), *A Clockwork Orange* (1971), *Barry Lyndon* (1975, shot by natural and candle light with special fast lenses), *The Shining* (1980, early extended Steadicam work), *Full Metal Jacket* (1987). Few titles, almost none minor, and a continuing influence on production design, music-and-image editing and visual effects. **Alternates:** Andrei Tarkovsky, David Lean.

### 4. Ingmar Bergman (TMDB 6648) - Sweden
Film as chamber psychology: *Smiles of a Summer Night* (1955), *The Seventh Seal* (1957), *Wild Strawberries* (1957), *Through a Glass Darkly* (1961), *Persona* (1966), *Cries and Whispers* (1972), *Scenes from a Marriage* (1973) and *Fanny and Alexander* (1982). Radical use of the close-up, long collaboration with cinematographer Sven Nykvist and a stock company of actors; three of his films won the Oscar for Best Foreign Language Film. Range from comedy to the most austere drama. **Alternates:** Carl Theodor Dreyer, Andrei Tarkovsky.

### 5. Federico Fellini (TMDB 4415) - Italy
Moved from neorealism (*I Vitelloni*, 1953; *La Strada*, 1954; *Nights of Cabiria*, 1957) into personal, dream-logic spectacle (*La Dolce Vita*, 1960, Palme d'Or; *8 1/2*, 1963; *Amarcord*, 1973). Four Foreign Language Oscars (*La Strada*, *Nights of Cabiria*, *8 1/2*, *Amarcord*), a film about making a film that became a model for self-portrait cinema, and an unmistakable visual vocabulary with composer Nino Rota. **Alternates:** Luchino Visconti, Michelangelo Antonioni.

### 6. Satyajit Ray (TMDB 12160) - India
*Pather Panchali* (1955), *Aparajito* (1956) and *Apur Sansar* (1959) form the Apu trilogy, one of cinema's great coming-of-age works, made on a tiny budget with non-professional actors. *The Music Room* (1958), *Devi* (1960), *Mahanagar* (1963), *Charulata* (1964) and *Days and Nights in the Forest* (1970) show range from period melancholy to urban modernity. He often composed the music and operated the camera himself, and received an honorary Oscar in 1992. **Alternates:** Ritwik Ghatak, Yasujiro Ozu.

### 7. Agnes Varda (TMDB 6817) - France
*La Pointe Courte* (1955) anticipated the New Wave; *Cleo from 5 to 7* (1962) plays out in real time through a Paris afternoon; *Le Bonheur* (1965) and *Vagabond* (1985, Golden Lion) use form to complicate emotion. Her documentaries *The Gleaners and I* (2000) and *Faces Places* (2017, co-directed with JR) are as significant as her fiction. Over sixty years of formal invention in both modes. **Alternates:** Francois Truffaut, Jean-Luc Godard, Jean Renoir. Note: several key films are documentaries or co-directed, which the ledger must handle explicitly.

### 8. Martin Scorsese (TMDB 1032) - United States
*Mean Streets* (1973), *Taxi Driver* (1976, Palme d'Or), *Raging Bull* (1980), *The King of Comedy* (1982), *After Hours* (1985), *Goodfellas* (1990), *The Age of Innocence* (1993), *Casino* (1995), *The Departed* (2006), *Silence* (2016) and *Killers of the Flower Moon* (2023). The most sustained run of high-quality work among major American directors; Thelma Schoonmaker's editing and the long Copacabana tracking shot in *Goodfellas* are craft touchstones. **Alternates:** Francis Ford Coppola, Sidney Lumet.

### 9. Steven Spielberg (TMDB 488) - United States
*Jaws* (1975) and *Raiders of the Lost Ark* (1981) redefined the popular film; *Close Encounters* (1977) and *E.T.* (1982) joined spectacle to feeling; *Schindler's List* (1993) and *Saving Private Ryan* (1998) won him Best Director Oscars; *Minority Report* (2002), *Munich* (2005) and *Lincoln* (2012) show continued range. Exceptional visual storytelling and staging with large casts and complex action. **Alternates:** Francis Ford Coppola, David Lean.

### 10. Hayao Miyazaki (TMDB 608) - Japan
*Nausicaa of the Valley of the Wind* (1984), *Castle in the Sky* (1986), *My Neighbor Totoro* (1988), *Kiki's Delivery Service* (1989), *Porco Rosso* (1992), *Princess Mononoke* (1997), *Spirited Away* (2001, Oscar for Best Animated Feature) and *The Boy and the Heron* (2023, also Best Animated Feature). Hand-drawn worlds with a consistent moral and visual sensibility and very few weak films. Note for ledger: TMDB lists shorts and TV episodes under his credits, and the US re-edit title *Warriors of the Wind* belongs to *Nausicaa*. **Alternates:** Isao Takahata, Satoshi Kon.

### 11. Wong Kar-wai (TMDB 12453) - Hong Kong
*Days of Being Wild* (1990), *Chungking Express* (1994), *Fallen Angels* (1995), *Happy Together* (1997, Best Director at Cannes), *In the Mood for Love* (2000) and *2046* (2004). Time, memory and longing expressed through step-printing, saturated colour and music; *In the Mood for Love* appears in the top five of the 2022 Sight and Sound critics' poll. Distinctive and widely imitated. **Alternates:** Edward Yang, Hou Hsiao-hsien.

### 12. Bong Joon-ho (TMDB 21684) - South Korea
*Memories of Murder* (2003), *The Host* (2006), *Mother* (2009), *Snowpiercer* (2013) and *Parasite* (2019) mix genre thrills, black comedy and class commentary within precise staging. *Parasite* won the Palme d'Or and the Oscars for Best Picture and Best Director. Fewest films of the twelve: roughly seven to eight strong features, so **his list may legitimately be shorter than ten**. **Alternates:** Park Chan-wook, Lee Chang-dong.

*Evidence caution: the Sight and Sound positions (Vertigo near the top, In the Mood for Love top five) were recalled from general knowledge and not re-confirmed this session; only Seven Samurai at number 20 in the 2022 critics' poll was seen on the BFI page. Confirm before quoting them in app copy.*

## Decisions for the owner

1. **Roster.** Approve these twelve or name swaps. My recommendation: approve as written. Biggest trade-off: it favours long, canonical careers over the most recognisable modern names. If household familiarity matters more than canon, swap in Nolan for Varda and Campion for Fellini, at the cost of narrower range.
2. **Scores in the app.** The approved layout shows IMDb and Rotten Tomatoes scores. Both services restrict copying and displaying their numbers and marks without a licence (details in the Essential 10 example). Options: (a) apply for licences, (b) link out to each page with no copied number, or (c) show a permitted alternative score. Recommendation: ship ranks and rationales first, and keep the score columns off until licences exist.
3. **Shorter lists.** Allow lists below ten where the work does not support ten (probably Bong; possibly Wong). Recommendation: yes, as the brief already permits.
4. **Documentaries and co-directed films** (Varda). Recommendation: include them when they are essential, flagged in the ledger.

### Rationale, one sentence each

1. **Schindler's List** - The Holocaust told in black and white with restraint and documentary-like images; won the Oscars for Best Picture and Best Director and the highest IMDb rating (9.0) in this lane.
2. **Jaws** - A thriller built around the absence of the shark, with edited point of view and John Williams's score; widely credited as the first summer blockbuster and the clearest demonstration of his command of suspense.
3. **Raiders of the Lost Ark** - Serial adventure rebuilt with modern staging; the standard for action-adventure choreography and clarity, with strong scores on both sources.
4. **E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial** - A suburban science-fiction fable told largely at a child's eye level; the highest Tomatometer in the lane (99%) and a defining popular film.
5. **Saving Private Ryan** - The D-Day landing sequence changed how battle is filmed (handheld camera, desaturated colour, sound design); second-highest IMDb rating in the lane (8.6) and a Best Director Oscar.
6. **Close Encounters of the Third Kind** - Awe and obsession in suburban America, built on light, sound and a contact sequence scored as a musical conversation; his most personal science-fiction film.
7. **Jurassic Park** - Joined animatronics and early digital effects in a way that reset the possibilities of spectacle cinema and set the model for the modern blockbuster.
8. **Catch Me If You Can** - A light, stylish caper with a 1960s design and title sequence; the strongest of his lighter films on both sources.
9. **Minority Report** - A noir-inflected science-fiction thriller with detailed production design and one of the most imitated user-interface concepts in film; shows his range after Schindler's List.
10. **Munich** - A morally uneasy thriller about the aftermath of the 1972 Olympic killings; the weakest numbers in the ten, kept as the most serious entry among his later work (see below).

## Ranking method

Applied exactly as in the Kurosawa worked example ([`ESSENTIAL_10_EXAMPLE.md`](./ESSENTIAL_10_EXAMPLE.md#ranking-method)): eligibility, longlist and evidence panel (at least 10,000 IMDb votes), each source read on its own terms (never averaged), tier by directing achievement, order within tier by editorial judgement, top three = Tier 1, freeze and version, second-reader review. Changes for this director: documentaries and co-directed films are allowed where essential and flagged (owner decision), and the list may be shorter than ten. Note that for the longlist films outside the ten, the RT score was read only where the film was a plausible candidate; the others carry an IMDb figure only.

### Where the evidence and the order disagree (all deliberate)

- **Saving Private Ryan has the second-highest IMDb rating (8.6) but is #5, and E.T. has the highest Tomatometer (99%) at #4, both behind Raiders of the Lost Ark (IMDb 8.4, RT 94%) at #3.** Reason: Raiders is placed third as the model for the modern action-adventure; Saving Private Ryan and E.T. are an easy swap and the third place is a genuine judgement call.
- **Jurassic Park (#7) and Catch Me If You Can (#8) score very high on IMDb (8.2 and 8.1)** and above Close Encounters (7.6, #6). Reason: Close Encounters outranks them on directorial ambition and influence; the order of 6-8 is a close call.
- **Munich (#10) has the weakest numbers in the ten (IMDb 7.5, RT 78%)** while Lincoln (RT 90%) and Bridge of Spies (RT 91%) beat it on the Tomatometer. Reason: kept as a major directing achievement of his late period. Closest call; Bridge of Spies is the alternate.
- **The ten contains none of his mid-1980s dramas (The Color Purple, Empire of the Sun) or his later biographical dramas apart from Munich**; the list leans toward suspense and spectacle, which is how the roster proposal describes his place in the canon.

**Known uncertainties**

- IMDb ids were matched in the IMDb dataset by original title, year and runtime, not read from a TMDB external_ids field; confirm with the TMDB API external_ids at implementation.
- RT review counts are a single read on the stated date via a text fetcher.
- IMDb title pages could not be read (HTTP 403); ratings and votes come from the official dataset (not cross-checked against the live pages), whose licence is non-commercial only.
- RT slug for Duel found by guess was a different 2013 film; its RT figures were not read and are shown as null.
- The TMDB person page displayed shifted years (see intro); the ledger uses original release years confirmed on IMDb dataset and RT pages.
- RT pages show US release years for some films; the ledger uses original release years (per-film notes in the JSON ledger).
- Review counts and vote counts move daily; these are snapshots.

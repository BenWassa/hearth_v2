### Rationale, one sentence each

1. **Parasite** - A tragicomic class thriller built on the precise layout of two houses and a mid-film change of genre; won the Palme d'Or and the Oscars for Best Picture and Best Director; top on both evidence lines (IMDb 8.5, RT 99% from 484 reviews).
2. **Memories of Murder** - A serial-killer procedural that becomes a portrait of a country under dictatorship and of the failure of method; among the most acclaimed Korean films and strong on both evidence lines.
3. **Mother** - A mystery in which a mother's devotion turns into something frightening; the most concentrated and compact film of his, with an RT score of 96% from 120 reviews.
4. **The Host** - A monster film with family comedy and political satire that became a major Korean box-office success and a model for his genre-mixing.
5. **Snowpiercer** - A science-fiction class allegory staged on one train; his first largely English-language film and strong critic standing, though lower on IMDb.
6. **Okja** - A girl-and-animal adventure with a satire of corporate food systems; a film that shows his tonal range but is more uneven.
7. **Barking Dogs Never Bite** - His debut, a black comedy about apartment-block neighbours; the earliest sign of his control of tone, included as the seventh and closest call (see below).

## Ranking method

Applied exactly as in the Kurosawa worked example ([`ESSENTIAL_10_EXAMPLE.md`](./ESSENTIAL_10_EXAMPLE.md#ranking-method)): eligibility, longlist and evidence panel (at least 10,000 IMDb votes), each source read on its own terms (never averaged), tier by directing achievement, order within tier by editorial judgement, top three = Tier 1, freeze and version, second-reader review. Changes for this director: documentaries and co-directed films are allowed where essential and flagged (owner decision), and the list may be shorter than ten. Note that for the longlist films outside the ten, the RT score was read only where the film was a plausible candidate; the others carry an IMDb figure only.

### Where the evidence and the order disagree (all deliberate)

- **Seven films, not ten.** Bong has eight features; Mickey 17 (IMDb 6.7, RT 77%) is the weakest on both lines and is left out. Padding to ten would require shorts or the Tokyo! segment, which are excluded. The owner approved lists shorter than ten.
- **The Host and Snowpiercer both have IMDb 7.1 (the lowest of the six main films) yet outrank Okja (IMDb 7.3, RT 87%).** Reason: both have stronger Tomatometer evidence (93% and 94%) and are more consequential to his reputation; Okja is the audience-score exception.
- **Barking Dogs Never Bite (#7) has IMDb 6.9 and RT 88% from only 17 reviews.** It is included as his debut and the earliest example of his style, but the numbers are weak and the owner might prefer a six-film list or Mickey 17 as #7. Closest call.
- **Mother (#3) has far fewer IMDb votes (82,353) than The Host (145,535) or Snowpiercer (427,503)** but a higher IMDb rating (7.7 vs 7.1 for both) and a higher Tomatometer than The Host (96% vs 93%). It is ranked on directing control; the third place is a judgement call between Mother and the more widely seen Snowpiercer.

**Known uncertainties**

- IMDb ids were matched in the IMDb dataset by original title, year and runtime, not read from a TMDB external_ids field; confirm with the TMDB API external_ids at implementation.
- RT review counts are a single read on the stated date via a text fetcher.
- IMDb title pages could not be read (HTTP 403); ratings and votes come from the official dataset (not cross-checked against the live pages), whose licence is non-commercial only.
- The IMDb id for the short Influenza was not looked up (null in the near-miss entry).
- RT pages show US release years for some films; the ledger uses original release years (per-film notes in the JSON ledger).
- Review counts and vote counts move daily; these are snapshots.

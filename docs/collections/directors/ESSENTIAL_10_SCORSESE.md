### Rationale, one sentence each

1. **GoodFellas** - A gangster life told from inside, with voice-over, freeze-frames and the Copacabana tracking shot turned into a method of narration; the highest IMDb rating in his filmography and the most-cited example of his editing with Thelma Schoonmaker.
2. **Taxi Driver** - Palme d'Or winner that turns a city at night into a character's mental state; defining 1970s American film and the template for the alienated urban protagonist.
3. **Raging Bull** - Boxing biography staged as the self-destruction of a man and filmed in black and white with subjective, stylised fight sequences; regularly named among the best American films of the 1980s.
4. **Mean Streets** - The breakthrough film: a personal, hand-held, rock-scored view of small-time criminals and Catholic guilt that fixed his themes and style; strong Tomatometer but the lowest IMDb rating in the ten (7.1).
5. **The Departed** - A tightly plotted crime thriller adapted from a Hong Kong film, and the work that brought him the Best Director and Best Picture Oscars; very high on both evidence lines.
6. **The King of Comedy** - A discomforting comedy of fame and obsession, misunderstood at release and now widely seen as prescient; a formal departure from his crime films.
7. **Casino** - Three-hour Las Vegas epic that pushes his GoodFellas methods (voice-over, montage, fast editing) toward operatic scale; high audience standing, mixed critic score, so kept below the films that score on both lines.
8. **The Last Waltz** - Concert documentary of The Band's farewell show, shot by a team of leading cinematographers and treated as a feature with staged interviews; the closest thing in his work to a pure formal experiment in capturing performance.
9. **After Hours** - A nightmare-comedy of one night downtown, made at speed on a low budget after a failed project; a showcase of invention in framing and pacing and a favourite of critics.
10. **Killers of the Flower Moon** - A late-career crime film about the murders of Osage people in 1920s Oklahoma, told with a slow, moral pace and a collaboration with editor Thelma Schoonmaker; the strongest recent film and the closest call (see below).

## Ranking method

Applied exactly as in the Kurosawa worked example ([`ESSENTIAL_10_EXAMPLE.md`](./ESSENTIAL_10_EXAMPLE.md#ranking-method)): eligibility, longlist and evidence panel (at least 10,000 IMDb votes), each source read on its own terms (never averaged), tier by directing achievement, order within tier by editorial judgement, top three = Tier 1, freeze and version, second-reader review. Changes for this director: documentaries and co-directed films are allowed where essential and flagged (owner decision), and the list may be shorter than ten. Note that for the longlist films outside the ten, the RT score was read only where the film was a plausible candidate; the others carry an IMDb figure only.

### Where the evidence and the order disagree (all deliberate)

- **GoodFellas (#1) is also the highest on IMDb (8.7), but Raging Bull (RT 92%) and Mean Streets (RT 92%) both score higher on the Tomatometer than Taxi Driver (89%).** Reason: Taxi Driver is ranked second on influence and canon standing; the 3-point gap is within normal variation.
- **The Departed has the second-highest IMDb rating (8.5, 1.59 million votes) but is #5.** Reason: the signature three are the films with the clearer claim on style and influence; The Departed is a remake and a genre exercise. The gap is deliberate and is the most likely to be moved up by an owner who prefers audience standing.
- **Shutter Island and The Wolf of Wall Street (IMDb 8.2 each) are outside the ten**, with RT scores of 69% and 79%. They equal or beat Casino (8.2, 78%) on IMDb. Casino is kept for the stronger directing case; this is a close call.
- **The Irishman (IMDb 7.8, RT 95% from 467 reviews) has better numbers on both sources than the #10, Killers of the Flower Moon (7.5, 93%).** Reason: Killers is a more formally distinct late work, while The Irishman repeats gangster ground covered by four other titles in the ten. Closest call; either order is defensible. Owner decision, see below.
- **Mean Streets (#4) has the lowest IMDb rating in the ten (7.1)**, kept for importance as the breakthrough and a 92% Tomatometer.

**Known uncertainties**

- IMDb ids were matched in the IMDb dataset by original title, year and runtime, not read from a TMDB external_ids field; confirm with the TMDB API external_ids at implementation.
- RT review counts are a single read on the stated date via a text fetcher.
- IMDb title pages could not be read (HTTP 403); ratings and votes come from the official dataset (not cross-checked against the live pages), whose licence is non-commercial only.
- Shutter Island RT review count was 260 on the page read and 262 in a search-result snapshot the same day; 260 is recorded.
- RT numbers for The King of Comedy, After Hours, The Age of Innocence, Casino, The Last Waltz and Shutter Island matched the percentage and count shown in search-result summaries as well as the film page; the Scorsese celebrity page on RT only showed recent titles, so the celebrity-table cross-check used for Kubrick, Miyazaki and Bong was not possible for most of Scorsese's list.
- RT pages show US release years for some films; the ledger uses original release years (per-film notes in the JSON ledger).
- Review counts and vote counts move daily; these are snapshots.

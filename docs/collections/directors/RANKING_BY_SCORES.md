# Legendary Directors: Essential 10 ranked by IMDb and Rotten Tomatoes

**Issue:** #60 · **Status:** draft for owner review · **Computed:** 2026-11-13 from the dated scores in each `essential10-<director>.json`

## Owner decision
Rankings are set by IMDb and Rotten Tomatoes scores, replacing the earlier editorial ordering. The earlier order is kept in each JSON file as `editorialRank`. The film selection per director is unchanged.

## Method
1. Within one director's list, rank the films by **IMDb rating** and, separately, by **Rotten Tomatoes critics Tomatometer**. Tied scores share the average rank.
2. Average the two **ranks** (not the raw scores, which measure different things).
3. Break ties by higher IMDb rating, then more IMDb votes.
4. A Tomatometer with fewer than 10 reviews, or none, is ignored; that film is ranked by IMDb alone and marked in the tables.
5. The top three are the signature films.

## What to know before relying on this
- Old and foreign-language films often have **small Rotten Tomatoes samples** (20 to 60 reviews), so a 100% there can outrank films with much larger audiences. This is why films such as Paths of Glory, Nights of Cabiria and Nayak rise. The 10-review floor is a dial: raising it to 30 would reduce the effect.
- IMDb figures come from IMDb's public data files read on 2026-11-13 (live pages blocked); Rotten Tomatoes figures were read per film page. See the score sourcing notes.
- Showing these numbers in the app is fine for a private household tool; a public release needs licences for IMDb and Rotten Tomatoes data and logos.
- **Score challengers** are films outside a director's ten that beat the tenth film on both IMDb and Rotten Tomatoes. Membership was not changed; the owner decides.
